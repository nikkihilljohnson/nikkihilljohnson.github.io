An air purification company and a school board candidate don't have much in common. But both need to give people a reason to pay attention. People share what makes them feel something, and they act when they trust whoever's asking.

So I start with the person on the other side of the screen: what they're worried about, what they'd forward to a friend, what would make them stop scrolling. Then I let the numbers check my work.

Sometimes that means a 33K-view video about South Carolina politics. Sometimes it means finding out that behind-the-scenes install footage beats every "book a free consult" post by a mile.

Brands

Boring categories are my favorite.

Air purifiers. HVAC. Countertops. Online education. Fitness. I've worked across industries that don't have much in common, except that they all need to give people a reason to pay attention.

Sometimes that means making a complicated product easier to understand. Sometimes it's finding the humor in something ordinary. And sometimes it's building a community around something people genuinely care about.

Organic gives people a reason to care. Paid makes sure more of them see it. You need both.

About

Elder millennial. Oregon Trail survivor.

I was making videos with a handheld camcorder in my basement before I even had internet. Turns out I've been making content my whole life. Twenty years and approximately a million algorithm changes later, I'm still finding new ways to get people to pay attention.

The short version.

I started at AOL in 2006, working in broadband marketing before moving into music and lifestyle content. Then I spent eight years at BODi building an online fitness community of more than 1,000 people. That taught me a lot about what makes people show up, stick around, and actually care.

From there, I worked in nonprofit marketing at Carolina Farm Trust, helped lead content for a $6.4 million launch with James Wedmore, and spent two years turning social media into a sales channel at HVAC.com.

In late 2025, I went out on my own. Now I work with brands that need experienced marketing leadership and Democratic candidates who need someone who understands both the message and how to get it in front of people.

I have a sociology degree, which explains a lot. I've always been more interested in why people do things than which app they're doing them on.

I live in the Charleston area with my husband, four kids, three cats, and a dog, which explains my comfort with organized chaos.
